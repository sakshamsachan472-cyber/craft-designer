Craft Designer Arjit Sachan

Rooted in tradition. Made for everyday life.

I am a craft designer inspired by traditional Indian art, especially Warli motifs and hand embroidery.

My work blends heritage with modern design through handmade shoes, textiles, and wearable pieces that feel meaningful and practical.

I aim to keep culture alive in everyday life by creating designs that are simple, thoughtful, and rooted in tradition.

My journeyHow I Found My Craft

“I started becoming interested in craft designing when I noticed how even the smallest motifs carry memories, culture, and emotion in them.”

— Arjit Sachan

“When I joined college, I began learning design more seriously, and that is where I understood how tradition can be reshaped into new stories.”

— College journey

“My first handmade piece was a small embroidered work inspired by Warli patterns. It felt simple, but it taught me how tradition can become modern.”

— First creation

“The idea of Warli shoes came when I wanted to carry the rhythm of tribal art into everyday life, where people could wear culture as a living expression.”

— Warli shoe concept

“Warli art feels like a quiet poem in geometry—circles, lines, and stories of home, harvest, dance, and togetherness, all moving gently in a single rhythm.”

— Cultural inspiration

About the Warli Art Shoes

Shoes Inspired by Warli Art and Culture

Warli Art is a tribal folk art form from Maharashtra, India, created by the Warli people.

One of the oldest styles of Indian art, dating back thousands of years

Known for its simple yet expressive geometric patterns.

Traditionally, Warli paintings were created on mud walls using rice paste, depicting scenes of daily life, nature, and community rituals.
